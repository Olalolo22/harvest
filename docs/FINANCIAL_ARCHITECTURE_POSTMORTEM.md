## 1. Executive Summary & Thesis Validation

The StockLana hackathon (604 project submissions, $126k pool) was designed to determine whether Solana can outcompete traditional prime brokerages by hosting tokenized US equities (xStocks by Backed Finance, PreStocks pre-IPO tokens). 

### 1.1 The Macro Thesis Was Validated
Harvest's core thesis—**writing covered-call options on tokenized equities to monetize idle stock holdings**—was validated at the highest level of competition:
- **1st Place Overall ($35,000):** *Roster Finance* built the exact same underlying primitive (fully collateralized options on tokenized stocks).
- The demand for non-linear structured yield products on tokenized real-world assets (RWAs) is proven and institutional.

### 1.2 The Failure Mode
Harvest was filtered out from podium consideration not because the Solana program failed to compile or run, but because of three structural weaknesses:
1. **Economic Vacuum in Financial Architecture:** V1 relied on an admin-funded USDC reserve and DEX liquidation instead of an actual counterparty (option buyer).
2. **Trust & Solvency Boundaries:** The smart contract enforced payout arithmetic but could not enforce economic solvency or executable DEX liquidity.
3. **Cognitive Model Friction (The "If-Else" Trap):** The frontend communicated the protocol as a 10-state derivative engine with split-brain settlement paths instead of a single, unified user intent.

This document conducts an unsparing autopsy of the V1 architecture and formalizes the blueprint for an institutional V2 primitive.

## 2. Core Financial Architecture Autopsy

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            THE V1 ECONOMIC SHORTCUTS                              |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                   |
|   1. Phantom Yield (No Option Buyer)                                              |
|      [Depositor NVDAx] ---> [Harvest Vault] <--- [Admin USDC Reserve]             |
|                                     │                     │                       |
|                                     ▼                     ▼                       |
|                          Pays fixed "premium" from admin capital                  |
|                                                                                   |
|   2. The DEX Dumping Fallacy (Swap != Option Exercise)                            |
|      If Price > Strike (ITM):                                                     |
|      [Vault NVDAx] ──(Jupiter Swap)──> [DEX AMM Pool] ──> [Realized USDC]         |
|                                               │                                   |
|                                       High Slippage &                             |
|                                       Illiquidity Risk                            |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1 Flaw 1: The "Phantom Yield" Problem (Admin Reserve vs. Counterparty)
In financial theory, covered-call yield is not "free money" generated by code. It is an **options premium** paid by an external counterparty who purchases asymmetric upside exposure (call delta) and volatility.

In Harvest V1:
- There was **no option buyer**.
- The protocol paid out a configured premium rate (e.g., 1.5% per 7-day cycle) directly from an administrative USDC reserve account (`vault_usdc_ata`).

#### Why This Breaks Financial Architecture
- **Contract Rules vs. Solvency:** Anchor instructions can enforce that `claim.rs` transfers `premium = user_shares * premium_rate`. However, the contract cannot guarantee that the admin reserve has enough USDC to honor those claims.
- **Economic Invalidation:** Paying yield out of an unbacked protocol reserve is indistinguishable from inflationary token printing or subsidized customer acquisition cost. Calling it an "options premium" was an economic misnomer.

---

### 2.2 Flaw 2: The DEX Liquidation Fallacy (Swap != Option Exercise)
In a conventional covered call:
```text
At Expiry T:
If S_T >= Strike (K):
    The option buyer exercises.
    Buyer transfers K * shares in cash to the seller.
    Buyer takes delivery of the shares.
    Seller receives exactly K * shares + Premium.
```

In Harvest V1:
- The keeper detected an ITM settlement (`settle_price_usd >= strike_price_usd`).
- Instead of settling with an option buyer, the keeper routed the underlying stock collateral through the **Jupiter DEX aggregator** to sell it into USDC on open liquidity pools (Raydium, Orca).

#### Why This Is Fatal for Tokenized Equities
1. **DEX Swapping Distorts the Payoff:** If NVDA rallies from $200 to $230 (strike $210), dumping 100% of the shares on Jupiter at $230 means the vault realizes $230 per share. The depositor never actually surrendered the upside above the strike to an option buyer! The option was never written.
2. **Oracle Benchmark vs. Executable Liquidity:** Pyth Hermes low-latency price feeds supply benchmark equity prices from NASDAQ. However, **tokenized stocks (xStocks) on Solana have shallow on-chain AMM pools**. 
   - Pyth can report NVDA at $215, but a $100,000 market sell on Jupiter might experience 8% to 15% slippage.
   - If the keeper executes with excessive slippage, depositors could receive less than the strike floor ($K$), suffering unhedged capital destruction.

*(Note: In commit `4991a51`, we patched this on-chain by introducing `compute_min_itm_proceeds()` to reject any keeper swap returning less than `strike_price * (1 - max_slippage)`. But the fundamental architectural issue—routing through AMMs rather than settling with a buyer—persisted).*

---

### 2.3 Flaw 3: Keeper Autonomy & Trust Boundaries
- **Liveness Dependency:** V1 depended on an off-chain keeper bot to call `lock_cycle` and `settle_*`. If the keeper went offline, ran out of SOL gas, or crashed during network congestion, vault capital remained locked with no user-executable fallback.
- **Front-Running & Settlement Timing:** Because the settlement window depended on keeper trigger timing, an adversarial keeper could delay `lock_cycle` or `settle_itm` to game oracle price fluctuations.

## 3. The Product Communication Gap (Jay's Critique)

Co-builder Jay identified the human-interface bottleneck:
> *"Harvest explains the mechanism, but doesn't make the benefit tangible to the user. We didn't showcase how a user benefits from all this."*

### 3.1 The "Blueprint on the Front Porch" Trap
Harvest presented users with an engine diagram:
- Strike distance calculations (Pyth price + 5%).
- Keeper cron loop status and rollover timestamps.
- Split-brain conditional branches:
  - *"If price < strike: you keep stock + USDC."*
  - *"If price >= strike: your stock is sold on Jupiter and you get USDC."*

This created **Assignment Shock**: depositors felt they were depositing stock for "yield," only to discover that on a massive rally, their stock was liquidated into dollars.

### 3.2 The 4-Stage Human-Centric Translation
A proper financial product must communicate via a 4-part progression:

```text
1. Financial Concept:
   "You get paid cash today for agreeing to sell your stock at a higher target price tomorrow."

2. The Strategy:
   "Deposit $1,000 of NVDAx at $100. Set a target exit at $110. Collect $20 in USDC upfront."

3. The User Benefit:
   "Put idle stock holdings to work earning recurring income instead of waiting passively for green candles."

4. The Demonstrable Outcome:
   - If NVDA stays below $110: You kept your NVDAx shares + kept the $20 USDC.
   - If NVDA crosses $110: Your limit sale executed at $110 (profit realized) + you kept the $20 USDC.
   Both outcomes fulfill the exact same user goal: Monetizing a Target-Exit Limit Order.
```

## 4. The Institutional V2 Architecture (The True Primitive)

To transition Harvest from a hackathon prototype into a production-grade institutional primitive, the architecture must eliminate internal subsidies and AMM liquidations.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        HARVEST V2: SOLVER & RFQ ARCHITECTURE                      |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                   |
|   1. Cycle Auction (The Option Buyer)                                             |
|      [Depositors (NVDAx)] ───► [Harvest Vault Escrow]                             |
|                                       │                                           |
|         ┌─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐             |
|         ▼                                                           ▼             |
|   [Solver A (Wintermute)]                                     [Solver B (Flow)]   |
|   Bids: $2.10 USDC / share                                    Bids: $2.40 USDC    |
|         │                                                           │             |
|         └─────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘             |
|                                       ▼ Winning Bid ($2.40)                       |
|                          Locks $2.40 USDC per share into Vault                    |
|                                                                                   |
|   2. Settlement: Zero AMM Slippage                                                |
|      - OTM (S_T < Strike):                                                        |
|        Depositors claim NVDAx principal + locked Solver USDC premium.             |
|                                                                                   |
|      - ITM (S_T >= Strike):                                                       |
|        Winning Solver deposits Full Strike Value in USDC (Strike * Shares).       |
|        Solver claims NVDAx PDA.                                                   |
|        Depositors claim Strike USDC + Solver USDC premium.                        |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 4.1 Shift 1: On-Chain Dutch Auction / RFQ for Option Rights
- **External Capital:** Harvest aggregates depositor xStock collateral into a single cycle batch.
- **Solver Bidding:** Registered market makers (e.g., Wintermute, Flow Traders, or decentralized solvers) bid USDC for the call option rights.
- **Guaranteed Solvency:** The cycle cannot lock until the winning solver's USDC premium is locked directly in the on-chain vault escrow. Payouts are 100% funded by market demand, not protocol reserves.

### 4.2 Shift 2: Physical Delivery / Strike Escrow (Zero DEX Dependency)
Harvest V2 eliminates Jupiter swaps entirely:
- **If Out-of-the-Money (`S_T < K`):** 
  - The option expires worthless.
  - The solver's locked USDC premium is distributed to depositors.
  - Depositors retain 100% of their xStock principal.
- **If In-the-Money (`S_T >= K`):**
  - The solver has the exclusive right to purchase the underlying xStocks at strike `K`.
  - To exercise, the solver must deposit `K * Total_Shares` in USDC into the vault escrow within a settlement grace period (e.g., 2 hours).
  - Upon solver deposit, the xStock PDA is released to the solver, and depositors claim their allocated USDC strike proceeds + premium.
  - **Result:** Zero AMM slippage, zero price impact, zero dependence on Raydium/Orca liquidity.

### 4.3 Shift 3: Non-Custodial Emergency Escape Hatch
- If a cycle is not settled within 24 hours of expiry (keeper and solver both fail), the vault enters `EmergencyExpired` state.
- Depositors can unilaterally call `emergency_withdraw()` to retrieve 100% of their deposited underlying xStocks directly from the PDA.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 6. Permanent Lessons for Future Primitives

1. **The Counterparty Invariant:** Never simulate yield with an internal protocol reserve or market sell. A financial primitive must have an external counterparty whose capital is locked upfront before user assets are exposed.
2. **The "No Conditional If/Else" Payoff Rule:** Never force users into a piecewise decision tree where asset denominations change unexpectedly. Reframe the product around a single desired outcome (e.g., Target Exit).
3. **The Trojan Horse Wedge:** Present one dead-simple use case in 5 seconds. Let judges and users step through that single door first—then reveal the depth of the protocol underneath.
